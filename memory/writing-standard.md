# Writing Standard

**Audience:** Indian independent hotel owner, 15 to 50 rooms, new to OTAs and
hotel software. Not a revenue manager, not a chain executive. Jargon and assumed
knowledge cause high bounce and zero conversions from search.

Run this checklist on every guide before commit.

## The checklist

1. **No unexplained jargon.** Explain every technical term in parentheses on
   first use:
   - "extranet (the OTA's partner dashboard where you manage your listing)"
   - "rate parity (keeping the same price across all booking channels)"
   - "AP/CP/MAP (meal plan types: American Plan, Continental Plan, Modified American Plan)"
   - "channel manager (software that syncs your availability across all OTAs at once)"

2. **Rates and numbers in ₹.** Never a bare percentage:
   - "18% commission" → "18% commission, and on a ₹3,000 booking that is ₹540"
   - Keep one base figure (₹3,000, ₹5,000) across a whole guide so the examples
     compare cleanly.
   - Currency is ₹ throughout. Never `$` or USD.

3. **Story first.** Open with a scene the reader recognises, not a definition:
   "You just listed on MMT and Booking.com. Now you need to update your rate for
   Diwali weekend…" Then the problem. Then the solution. New hoteliers do not yet
   recognise the pain points, so show them before naming them.

4. **Short sentences.** 25 words maximum. Use full stops, not commas, to break a
   long thought.

5. **"You" framing.** "Your hotel", "your guests", "you will save". Not "hotels
   should" or "properties can".

6. **8th grade reading level.** "Stop" not "cease". "Start" not "initiate".
   "Use" not "utilise".

7. **Bullets over prose.** Any section covering 3 or more related points gets a
   `<ul>` or `<ol>`. Reserve prose for 1-2 sentences of context before the
   bullets. Never write four sentences where four bullets would do.

8. **Bold key concepts on first use in a section**, with `<strong>`, not
   throughout. `<strong>channel manager</strong>` is the most important term on
   the site and works especially well in the Quick Answer Box and in the intro
   to the section that explains it. Bolding every instance in every bullet reads
   as keyword stuffing, so first-use-per-section is the rule.

9. **"New to X? Start here" callout** at the top of every advanced guide,
   linking to the foundational guide for that topic.

10. **No em dashes (—).** Overuse is a strong tell of AI-written text and it
    breaks the conversational tone. Replace with a full stop and new sentence, a
    colon, a comma, or parentheses. This applies to body copy, bullets,
    highlight boxes, FAQs, titles and meta descriptions. Check before publishing;
    a bulk removal has already had to run twice, and it broke number ranges the
    first time (`5–10` became `5 10`), so re-read what a script rewrites.
