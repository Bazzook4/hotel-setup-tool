Avoid the em dash (—) character in guide content. Its overuse is a strong signal of AI-generated text and the user has explicitly flagged it as feeling unnatural.

**Why:** User identified — as a giveaway that content is AI-written. It breaks the human, conversational tone the brand aims for.

**How to apply:** Replace — with: a period and new sentence, a colon, a comma, or plain brackets. Never use — in body copy, bullet points, highlight boxes, or FAQs. Check every piece of content before publishing.

"Channel manager" (and other key product/concept terms) should be given visual emphasis — use `<strong>` tags the first time the term appears in a section, not throughout. Makes the page feel intentional rather than keyword-stuffed.

**Why:** User asked for emphasis on "channel manager" as the key concept. Strong tags signal importance to readers scanning the page.

**How to apply:** Bold the term on first use in the body (not in every bullet). Works especially well in the Quick Answer Box and the intro paragraph of the section that explains what it does.

---

Use bullet points to explain multiple points within a section, not long prose paragraphs. Bullet points are easier to scan and more readable for hotel owners who are time-poor.

**Why:** User explicitly asked for bullet points over paragraphs when listing tips, steps, or multiple related ideas. Prose paragraphs feel heavy and slow to read.

**How to apply:** Any time a section covers 3 or more related points, use a `<ul>` or `<ol>`. Reserve prose paragraphs for context-setting (1-2 sentences max before the bullets). Never write 4+ sentence paragraphs to explain something that could be 4 bullets.

---

Content must tell a story that connects to the reader's real situation before introducing concepts or solutions. New hoteliers need to see their own experience reflected before they trust the advice.

**Why:** User felt the guide jumped too quickly into problem statements and solution features without building a relatable story first. New hoteliers don't yet recognise the pain points being described — they need to be shown it first.

**How to apply:** Open with a scenario the target reader will recognise (e.g., "You just listed on MMT and Booking.com. Now you need to update your rate for Diwali weekend..."). Then introduce the problem. Then the solution. Story-first, solution-second.
