GST Rules for Hotels: ITC, Filing & Invoices

By OnlineHotelier Insights Team 9 min read

Hotel GST at a Glance

Rates, ITC, invoices, filing.
Room price GST rate ITC

For worked examples and CGST/SGST rules, see the full GST rate guide for hotel rooms. This page covers ITC, invoices, filing and OTA commission GST.

Your MMT settlement lands in the bank account and three lines are missing from what you expected: TDS, TCS and an OTA commission invoice with 18% GST. Your accountant asks for the GSTR-1 data but you are not sure which sales go where. These are the hotel GST rules questions this guide answers: when you can claim ITC, what a valid invoice must show, which return to file and by when, and how OTA commission GST works.

GST Rates for Hotels: Quick Summary

Rooms priced up to ₹7,500 per night: 5% GST, no ITC. Rooms above ₹7,500: 18% GST, ITC available. Both slabs are based on the actual price charged to the guest. For slab examples, lodge and resort rates, F&B rules and worked calculations see the full GST rate guide for hotel rooms.

Input Tax Credit (ITC) for Hotels: What You Can Claim

Input Tax Credit (ITC) lets you offset GST paid on purchases against GST owed on room revenue. Hotels in the 5% slab cannot claim it at all.

ITC-eligible expenses:

  • Furniture, fixtures, equipment, linen and room amenities
  • Technology: PMS, booking engine and channel manager subscriptions
  • Professional services: accounting, legal, marketing
  • Maintenance and housekeeping contracts
  • OTA commission invoices (18% GST on commission, Indian OTAs)

ITC is blocked on: staff food, personal accommodation, motor vehicles and any inputs used exclusively for 5% room categories (Section 17(5) CGST Act).

The 5% rooms problem: If you operate rooms in both slabs, you must split ITC proportionately under Rule 42. Common expenses such as power and housekeeping must be apportioned by taxable turnover. Your accountant should set this up.

ITC on Hotel Stay: Business Traveller Claims

A GST-registered business can claim ITC on an employee's hotel stay only when all four conditions are met:

  1. The room was charged at 18% GST, i.e. the actual price per night was above ₹7,500. Rooms in the 5% slab carry no ITC for either party.
  2. The hotel issues a proper B2B tax invoice with the company's GSTIN on it.
  3. The stay is for a genuine business purpose, not a personal trip.
  4. The stay is not in a blocked category under Section 17(5).

If any condition fails, the ITC is blocked. Common failures: the room was in the 5% slab (no ITC for any party), or the hotel issued a B2C receipt because the guest did not give their company GSTIN at check-in. Hotel accommodation is always taxed as CGST plus SGST of the hotel's state, so a business can claim it only through a GST registration in that state. Otherwise the GST is a cost.

Front desk tip: Ask every corporate guest for their company GSTIN at check-in. Once issued without it, correcting the invoice requires a credit note. Many companies lose ITC simply because the hotel did not ask.

OTA bookings: The guest still needs the hotel's B2B tax invoice for ITC. Make sure your PMS can issue one in the company's name even on OTA bookings.

GST Invoice Requirements for Hotels

Every GST invoice from your hotel must contain these fields. A missing field makes it non-compliant and blocks ITC for the recipient.

  1. Hotel name, address and GSTIN: Exactly as registered in GST records.
  2. Sequential invoice number: A unique series. Gaps or resets create audit issues.
  3. Invoice date: Typically the check-out date for accommodation.
  4. Guest or company name, address and GSTIN: Required for B2B invoices; name and address only for B2C.
  5. Taxable value: The room rate before GST.
  6. GST breakdown: CGST plus SGST, shown separately. SAC code 9963 applies to all hotel accommodation and F&B services.
  7. Total amount: Taxable value plus GST.
SAC code 9963 covers all hotel accommodation and F&B services. Older sources may say HSN 9963; both are the same code. Use SAC 9963 on every invoice.

Place of Supply: CGST/SGST vs IGST

Hotel accommodation always uses the hotel's location as the place of supply, under Section 12(3) of the IGST Act. This applies regardless of where the guest is from: a Delhi guest staying in a Jaipur hotel is taxed under CGST plus Rajasthan SGST, not IGST. Restaurant and room service follow the same rule.

Never charge IGST on a hotel room bill. Even for a corporate client from another state, the invoice must show CGST plus your state's SGST.

GST Filing: GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-9

GST-registered hotels must file three returns. Missing a deadline triggers a penalty and interest.

Return What it covers Frequency Due date
GSTR-1 Details of all outward supplies (your sales) Monthly 11th of the next month
GSTR-3B Summary return with tax payment Monthly 20th of the next month
GSTR-9 Annual consolidated return Yearly 31 December of the following year

Hotels with annual turnover below ₹5 crore can opt for the QRMP (Quarterly Return Monthly Payment) scheme: quarterly GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B with monthly payments via PMT-06.

Late filing penalty: GSTR-3B filed late attracts ₹50 per day (₹20 per day for a nil return). Interest accrues at 18% per annum on any outstanding tax from the due date. File on time even if you cannot pay the full amount; the penalty clock runs on the return itself, not the payment.

Hotels on OTAs must also reconcile TCS deducted by each OTA. This credit appears in your GSTR-2A/2B and should be matched monthly. See the TDS & TCS guide.

GST on OTA Commissions

OTAs charge hotels commission for bookings. This commission is a service, and GST applies at 18%. The rules differ slightly between Indian and foreign OTAs.

Indian OTAs: MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, Booking.com India

Indian OTAs issue a tax invoice with 18% GST on their commission. A GST-registered hotel receives this invoice and can claim the GST as ITC against its own output liability.

Example: Booking.com booking at ₹5,000 with 15% commission

Booking amount: ₹5,000

OTA commission (15%): ₹750

GST on commission (18%): ₹135

OTA deducts: ₹750 + ₹135 = ₹885

Hotel receives: ₹5,000 - ₹885 = ₹4,115 (before TCS deduction)

ITC available to hotel: ₹135 (claiming this reduces your GST payable)

Foreign OTAs: Agoda, Expedia, Airbnb (billed from overseas)

When a foreign OTA bills you from outside India, no Indian GST appears on their invoice. Instead, you must account for the tax yourself under the Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM).

Under RCM:

  • You calculate 18% IGST on the foreign OTA's commission amount.
  • You pay this IGST directly to the government (not to the OTA).
  • You can then claim this same amount back as ITC in the following tax period, subject to normal ITC conditions.
  • Declare this in Table 3.1(d) of GSTR-3B as RCM supplies.

Example: Agoda booking, ₹8,000 room, 15% commission

Booking amount: ₹8,000

Agoda commission (15%): ₹1,200

IGST under RCM (18% on ₹1,200): ₹216

You pay ₹216 to GST portal (not to Agoda)

ITC you can claim next period: ₹216

Trigger: Agoda invoices say "billing from Singapore" or similar. Keep foreign OTA invoices and match them monthly.

Common GST Mistakes by Hotels

  1. Applying the wrong slab: Charging 18% on rooms priced below ₹7,500, or forgetting to move to 18% when rates cross the threshold during peak season.
  2. Issuing B2C receipts for corporate guests: If the front desk does not collect the company GSTIN, the guest loses ITC and disputes the invoice. A corrected invoice with a credit note creates paperwork and delays.
  3. Charging IGST on room bills: Hotel accommodation place of supply is always the hotel's state. The tax is always CGST plus SGST, never IGST.
  4. Missing ITC reversals under Rule 42: Hotels with both 5% and 18% room categories must reverse a proportionate share of ITC on common expenses. This creates a shortfall in GSTR-9 if ignored.
  5. Missing RCM for foreign OTAs: Agoda and other OTAs billing from outside India require you to self-declare 18% IGST under reverse charge. Many hotels skip this entirely, which surfaces during assessment.
Use hotel accounting software: Multiple slabs, ITC reversals, RCM entries and OTA reconciliation are error-prone to manage manually. A hotel accounting package built for the Indian market can automate these and generate GSTR-ready reports.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a hotel owner claim ITC on their own hotel stay?
No. Section 17(5)(b) of the CGST Act blocks ITC on accommodation and food when used for personal consumption. A hotel owner staying at their own or any other hotel for personal purposes cannot claim ITC, even if a proper GST invoice is issued.
Can a business traveller claim ITC on a hotel bill?
A GST-registered business can claim ITC on an employee's hotel bill if: the room was charged at 18% GST (actual price per night above ₹7,500, as 5% slab rooms carry no ITC for either party); the hotel issued a B2B tax invoice with the company's GSTIN; the stay was for genuine business; and the stay was not blocked under Section 17(5). Note: hotel GST is always CGST plus the hotel's state SGST. A recipient can only offset this ITC if they are GST-registered in that state.
What are the mandatory fields on a hotel GST invoice?
A valid hotel GST invoice must include: hotel name, address and GSTIN; sequential invoice number; invoice date; guest name, address and GSTIN (for B2B bookings); SAC code 9963; taxable value; GST breakdown as CGST plus SGST; total amount; and place of supply (the state where the hotel is located).
What is the HSN/SAC code for hotel accommodation?
Hotel accommodation uses SAC 9963. This covers accommodation and food and beverage services from hotel premises. SAC codes replace HSN codes for services; SAC 9963 is the correct reference for both accommodation and restaurant services.
Does GST apply to OTA commissions charged to hotels?
Yes. Indian OTAs such as MakeMyTrip, Goibibo and Booking.com charge 18% GST on their commission and issue a tax invoice to the hotel. A GST-registered hotel can claim this GST as ITC. For example, on a ₹5,000 booking with a 15% commission (₹750), the OTA bills ₹750 plus ₹135 GST. The hotel claims ₹135 as ITC. Foreign OTAs such as Agoda (billed from overseas) require the hotel to self-account under reverse charge at 18% IGST.
Do small hotels below ₹20 lakh turnover need to register for GST?
Hotels below ₹20 lakh turnover (₹10 lakh in special category states) are not required to register. However, any hotel receiving OTA bookings should register regardless of turnover. OTAs deduct TCS at 0.5% under Section 52 of the CGST Act, and claiming that credit requires an active GSTIN.
What GST returns must hotels file each month?
Monthly filers must file GSTR-1 by the 11th and GSTR-3B by the 20th of each month, plus GSTR-9 annually by 31 December. Hotels below ₹5 crore turnover can switch to quarterly GSTR-1 filing under the QRMP scheme.
What happens if a hotel files GSTR-3B late?
Late filing attracts ₹50 per day (₹20 per day for a nil return) plus 18% per annum interest on unpaid tax. File on time even if you cannot pay: the penalty clock starts from the due date, not the payment date.
